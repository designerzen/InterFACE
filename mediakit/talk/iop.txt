--- Automation & Helping Technology

For as long as there has been technological help, there have been people upset about it.

[CLICK - etching of Ned Ludd]

In the early 1800s, nearby to Manchester -- birthplace of the industrial revolution, a group of disenfranchised textile workers, led by Ned Ludd, systematically destroyed the machinery that was brought in to increase productivity. The looms and mills were vandelised beyond the point of repair. This pushback or, "collective bargaining", was powerful, but it was not the machines that the Luddites disagreed with, but how it required more demanding labour for the same pay as the reduced price per product meant production had to exponentially increase. 

They were not anti-technology or anti-progress, they just required a gradual transition.


[CLICK - newspaper about calculators]

A robot should admit when they do not know something. 

However, as most of these models are commercial, their success depends on the loyalty and trust of their users to spend their money and attract more investment. If that model was honest and told you that it was not sure, or that it did not know, the user would likely try another model, and may in fact prefer this less honest model, not for the quality of the answer, but for its reassuring confidence.
